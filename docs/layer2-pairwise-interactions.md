# Continua — Layer 2: Pairwise Interaction Reference

**Status:** Draft for issue #28 (Author Layer 2 — pairwise interaction reference)
**Register:** Internal / "therapist's reference." Not user-facing prose — this is scaffolding for the composition engine, written in a clinical-but-humane register consistent with the book's own voice.
**Naming convention:** Entries describe behavior patterns and mechanisms only — no named public figures. Layer 2 feeds the composition engine that generates a real person's report (Layers 4/5), so anchors are written to be safe to paraphrase into that output without ever comparing an assessment-taker to a named individual, living or dead.
**Governing principle ("interpolate, not classify"):** Each pair below names four *anchor points* in a continuous two-axis space, not four discrete personality types. A real profile can sit anywhere in the space these four anchors bound — closer to one corner, near an edge, or near the center where none of the four descriptions applies cleanly. The composition engine should treat these as reference poles for interpolation, and should not force a person into the nearest of the four.

**Axis pole key** (used throughout):
- Social Attunement (SA): Hyper-Attuned ↔ Hypo-Attuned
- Empathy (EM): Empathy ↔ Detachment
- Self-Orientation (SO): Altruism ↔ Self-Focus
- Conscientiousness (CO): Conscientious ↔ Impulsive
- Agency (AG): Agentic ↔ Accommodating
- Reactivity (RE): High-Reactive ↔ Low-Reactive

---

## 1. Social Attunement × Empathy

**Hyper-Attuned + Empathy — "the read and the response"**
The perceptual scan (micro-expressions, vocal shifts, unspoken tension) is not just received but *matters*: each signal registers as something to act on rather than merely notice. This produces the "people feel genuinely seen" effect the book associates with high attunement, but with an added layer of felt concern, not just accurate perception. Risk profile: absorption — high input plus high moral pull can leave little bandwidth left over for the self.

**Hypo-Attuned + Detachment — "flat terrain"**
Low signal in, low pull to respond even to what little comes through. Not dramatic coldness — closer to "a perfectly pleasant evening" without noticing there were undercurrents to miss, compounded by the fact that even a pointed-out undercurrent wouldn't generate much motivational charge. Reads as obliviousness rather than indifference, since there's no evidence of suppressed concern — the concern simply isn't being recruited.

**Hyper-Attuned + Detachment — "precision without concern"**
The book names this directly: the con artist reads every signal with exceptional accuracy and uses what's read purely as leverage. The perceptual instrument is fully online; the caring instrument is not. Easy to mistake for the first shape from the outside — accuracy of the read can look like warmth — which is why betrayals by socially skilled people land so hard ("but they were so nice").

**Hypo-Attuned + Empathy — "caring for a signal you can't see"**
Genuine moral concern is active, but the perceptual input that would normally trigger it is thin. Produces a sympathetic failure mode: the person would care, and does care in general, but doesn't reliably notice the specific moment that calls for it. An explicit prompt ("I need you right now") reliably produces real engagement — distinguishing this from Hypo-Attuned + Detachment, where the same prompt would not.

## 2. Self-Orientation × Agency

**Altruism + Agentic — "the directive server"**
Resources and priority flow outward, pursued assertively rather than passively — initiating structures or interventions on others' behalf rather than waiting for a request. Generosity here tends to be systematic and institution-building rather than personal and case-by-case. Risk: paternalism — assertiveness in service of someone else's interest can still override what that person actually wants ("doing things for people rather than with them").

**Self-Focus + Accommodating — "the withdrawn self"**
Attention centers on the self, but expresses as withdrawal rather than assertion — hypersensitivity to criticism, retreat, a more concealed sense of entitlement. The book's vulnerable-narcissism anchor rather than the grandiose one. Can be mistaken for accommodation-as-altruism from outside; the distinguishing feature is that the withdrawal serves self-protection, not concern for the other party.

**Self-Focus + Agentic — "the architecture of self, asserted"**
The grandiose-narcissism anchor: attention and resources concentrate on the self, pursued confidently and directively — a singular personal vision that others are expected to execute, held with intense concern for how it will be remembered. The book is explicit that ordinary high self-orientation plus agency (ambition, independence, competitiveness) is normal here; the pathological edge appears specifically when other people's interests stop registering as a constraint at all.

**Altruism + Accommodating — "service without a seat at the table"**
Other-directed motivation expressed through support rather than initiative — managing, stabilizing, absorbing risk on someone else's behalf, avoiding the spotlight rather than claiming it. Real, sustained generosity delivered by backing someone else's agenda rather than asserting one's own. Risk: invisibility — the contribution can be structurally essential and socially unrecognized at once.

## 3. Conscientiousness × Reactivity

**Conscientious + High-Reactive — "disciplined intensity"**
Strong emotional amplitude moves through structure rather than around it — planning and follow-through contain what would otherwise be volatile. Intensity shows up in the drive behind the work rather than in the reliability of the work itself.

**Impulsive + Low-Reactive — "easy drift"**
Low structure, low amplitude: decisions shift readily, but not because anything is emotionally charged — there's simply little friction holding a course in place. Reads as easygoing rather than chaotic; distinguish sharply from the fourth shape below, which shares the impulsivity but not the calm.

**Conscientious + Low-Reactive — "the steady hand"**
Structure without emotional amplification — closest to what most organizations describe as a reliable operator: plans hold, follow-through is consistent, none of it driven or disrupted by strong feeling. A disciplined, maintained regimen — the body and career treated as things to sustain rather than simply use up — is the book's pattern for this end.

**Impulsive + High-Reactive — "combustible spontaneity"**
Low structural containment plus high emotional amplitude — chasing an impulse in the moment, with no plan and no safety net under it. The highest-volatility combination on this pair: real creative or interpersonal intensity, with the fewest internal guardrails once emotion moves it. Complementarity with a high-conscientiousness partner (structure supplied externally) is a recurring pattern the book associates with this anchor.

## 4. Empathy × Self-Orientation

**Empathy + Altruism — "the felt calling"**
The central high-high anchor: felt resonance with others' suffering paired with a life whose priorities have genuinely reorganized around service. Suffering registers as moral pain and generosity flows as an almost automatic response. Risk: the "selfless giver" pattern the book describes — generosity without adequate self-protection, ending in exhaustion or resentment.

**Detachment + Self-Focus — "the closed loop"**
Neither perceiving others' suffering as a pull nor directing resources outward. The most self-contained anchor on this pair — not necessarily hostile, simply organized entirely around the self's own concerns, with no countervailing moral or motivational force from either axis pulling attention elsewhere.

**Detachment + Altruism — "principled giving"**
A pattern the book documents explicitly: giving driven by reasoned principle rather than felt anguish — compelled by an argument about what produces the best outcome, not by visceral distress over a stranger's suffering. The giving is real; the fuel is deliberative rather than emotional. Distinguish from Empathy + Altruism by asking what would happen if the emotional pull were absent — here, the giving continues unchanged, because it was never running on that pull to begin with.

**Empathy + Self-Focus — "moved but not moved to act"**
Genuine emotional resonance with others' suffering that does not translate into where resources or priority actually flow. This is a harder anchor to spot than the others because the felt concern is authentic — the gap is specifically at the point where feeling would need to become sacrifice, and the self-orientation wins that contest reliably. Reads to others as inconsistency ("you seemed so moved — why didn't you help?") rather than as coldness.

## 5. Empathy × Conscientiousness

**Empathy + Conscientious — "the sustained caregiver"**
Moral concern for others is channeled through structure rather than dissipating in the moment — commitments to help are followed through on schedule, over time, not just in the emotional peak when the need was first felt. This is the profile most likely to build durable caregiving systems (recurring donations, consistent presence, reliable follow-up) rather than one-off responses to acute appeals.

**Detachment + Impulsive — "the uncommitted moment"**
Low moral pull and low structural follow-through combine to produce minimal, unpredictable engagement with others' needs — not from active refusal, but because neither the caring nor the discipline is present to generate or sustain a response. When help is given here, it tends to be spontaneous and situational rather than principled or planned.

**Empathy + Impulsive — "the responsive but unreliable helper"**
The moral pull is real and can be intense in the moment — dropping everything for a friend's crisis — but lacks the structural follow-through to sustain it once the acute moment passes. This produces a specific relational friction: people the person has helped may feel the help was genuine but incomplete, because the plan for what comes next never solidified.

**Detachment + Conscientious — "the reliable non-responder"**
Structure and follow-through are strong, but not organized around others' emotional needs — commitments are honored, systems maintained, but not because someone's suffering created a pull to act. This can look, from a distance, like dependable support; up close, the reliability is about the system, not about the person being helped.

## 6. Empathy × Agency

**Empathy + Agentic — "the advocate"**
Moral concern combines with a willingness to act on it directly and assertively — initiating on someone else's behalf rather than waiting to be asked, intervening in conflict, pushing for a resource or a change because someone's suffering demands it. This is close to the book's description of the hyper-attuned mediator's cousin: not detecting tension (that's Social Attunement) but acting decisively once genuine need is recognized.

**Detachment + Accommodating — "the passive bystander"**
Neither the moral pull nor the assertive instinct is present. Not cruelty — closer to genuine non-involvement: a low likelihood of either noticing an obligation to act or pushing to act on it even if pointed out. The book's low-agency description ("assertion feels dangerous") compounds with detachment's lack of motivational charge to produce the least interventionist anchor on this pair.

**Empathy + Accommodating — "the quiet carrier"**
Real, sometimes intense concern for others' suffering, but expressed through support and deference rather than direct intervention — absorbing others' distress, adjusting one's own plans, without asserting what the situation might actually require. Risk: the person can end up carrying significant emotional weight for others while having little influence over the conditions causing it, since assertion itself feels costly.

**Detachment + Agentic — "the book's own named danger"**
The combination the book flags explicitly as producing "predatory dynamics": directive, confident, comfortable with power, and unmoved by the cost that direction imposes on others. Not every instance is predatory — low empathy plus agency can also simply mean decisive leadership that doesn't need emotional buy-in to function — but this is the anchor where the risk is highest and should be weighted accordingly in any composition that surfaces this pair.

## 7. Empathy × Reactivity

**Empathy + High-Reactive — "the flooded responder"**
Moral concern for others' suffering combines with a nervous system that amplifies whatever it registers — the felt pull to help arrives not as a quiet obligation but as urgent, sometimes overwhelming pressure. The book's discussion of high-reactivity vulnerability (guilt trips landing because the guilt is genuinely, viscerally felt) applies with particular force here, since the empathic pull gives manipulators a specific lever to pull.

**Detachment + Low-Reactive — "the even keel"**
Low moral pull and low amplitude combine into a profile that is difficult to move on either register — neither the suffering of others nor much else registers with much force. This is the anchor furthest from urgency of any kind on this pair; useful in crisis management precisely because nothing here escalates, at some cost to warmth.

**Empathy + Low-Reactive — "the steady advocate"**
Genuine moral concern paired with a nervous system that doesn't flood — the person who cares as much as the highly reactive empathizer but can sit with someone's pain, or act on a cause, without being overwhelmed by it. The book associates this pattern with sustained effectiveness in caregiving or advocacy roles precisely because burnout risk is lower.

**Detachment + High-Reactive — "intensity without a moral compass"**
Amplitude is high, but it isn't being generated by others' suffering — the person can be volatile, passionate, easily activated, while that activation runs on its own internal logic rather than on empathic concern. From outside this can be mistaken for empathy (visible emotional intensity reads as caring), but the book's distinction holds: Reactivity describes amplitude, not what the amplitude is about.

## 8. Social Attunement × Self-Orientation

**Hyper-Attuned + Altruism — "the attentive giver"**
Rich social perception feeds directly into where resources and priority flow — this person doesn't just want to help, they accurately perceive who needs it and how, because the antenna is picking up the signal that the motivation then acts on. The book's description of hyper-attuned mediators (understanding not just what people say but what they need) combines here with genuine other-directed priority.

**Hypo-Attuned + Self-Focus — "unaware and unmoved"**
Neither perceiving others' circumstances in detail nor prioritizing them if perceived. This is a low-friction anchor in one specific sense — the person isn't withholding attention from others so much as simply not generating much of either the perceptual or motivational material that would direct it there.

**Hyper-Attuned + Self-Focus — "the instrumentalized read"**
Fine-grained social perception in service of self-directed goals — reading a room accurately and using that read for personal advantage, status, or control, echoing the book's con-artist anchor but generalized beyond Empathy specifically. The perceptual gift is intact; what it's aimed at is the self's own position.

**Hypo-Attuned + Altruism — "care without a map"**
Strong other-directed motivation without the fine-grained perception to reliably know how to apply it. The intent to help is genuine and can be structural or systematic (echoing the book's altruism-through-systems examples), but the person is more likely to miss the specific, situational cues that would tell them help is currently needed, or in what form.

## 9. Social Attunement × Conscientiousness

**Hyper-Attuned + Conscientious — "the calibrated planner"**
Rich social input feeds into disciplined, structured follow-through — plans and systems are built with accurate awareness of the social terrain they'll operate in, rather than in isolation from it. This is a strong anchor for effective mediation and negotiation infrastructure: the read informs the plan, and the plan is actually executed.

**Hypo-Attuned + Impulsive — "the double blind spot"**
Neither the social signal nor the structural follow-through is strong. Plans shift without much awareness of the social consequences of the shift — not from disregard, but because the information that would flag a problem (social) and the discipline that would correct for it (structural) are both thin. This is the anchor most prone to blindsiding others without any intent to do so.

**Hyper-Attuned + Impulsive — "reads the room, changes the plan anyway"**
Accurate, real-time social perception paired with low structural commitment — the person notices exactly what's shifting in a room or relationship and adapts fluidly, sometimes to the frustration of more conscientious collaborators who experience the resulting changes as unreliability rather than responsiveness. The book's "adaptive attunement" middle-ground language is relevant context here, though this anchor sits at the impulsive extreme rather than the balanced center.

**Hypo-Attuned + Conscientious — "the systems-first operator"**
Low social input, high structural discipline — plans and systems are built and executed reliably, but with less awareness of, or accommodation for, the social terrain they land in. The book's description of hypo-attunement finding "greater cognitive resources available for systems, patterns, and details" pairs naturally with conscientiousness here, often producing technically excellent execution that requires a more socially attuned partner or collaborator to land well with people.

## 10. Social Attunement × Agency

**Hyper-Attuned + Agentic — "calibrated influence"**
Real-time social calibration combined with directive, confident assertion — reading exactly which pressure point moves a given listener, and pressing it, sometimes bending facts to fit the purpose at hand. Distinct from Empathy + Agentic (the advocate) because the driving force here is accurate perception in service of influence, not moral concern for the other person's wellbeing.

**Hypo-Attuned + Accommodating — "quiet and unaware"**
Low social perception and low assertive instinct combine into a profile that neither reads the room nor tries to steer it — comfortable receding, and largely unaware of what it might mean to lead even if the opportunity arose. Low-friction in group settings, but also the anchor least likely to notice when its own accommodation is being exploited.

**Hyper-Attuned + Accommodating — "the informed non-leader"**
Accurate, fine-grained social perception that does not translate into assertion — the person may see exactly what a group needs, who's uncomfortable, where the tension sits, but experiences directly acting on that information as costly or unnatural. Often ends up functioning as an informal advisor to whoever does lead, supplying the read without claiming the authority.

**Hypo-Attuned + Agentic — "blunt initiative"**
The opposite pairing: low social read paired with comfortable, direct assertion — explicit logic and direct, transactional interaction rather than socially calibrated persuasion. Confidence and initiative are present, but not calibrated to social nuance; influence here comes from clarity and directness rather than precision-targeted reading of the listener.

## 11. Social Attunement × Reactivity

**Hyper-Attuned + High-Reactive — "intensely engaged"**
The book names this combination directly: someone receiving an enormous amount of social information and responding to all of it at high amplitude — "intensely engaged, emotionally expressive, perhaps charismatic, and sometimes exhausting," both to the person and to those around them.

**Hypo-Attuned + Low-Reactive — "the quiet operator"**
Low social input, low amplitude — the anchor least stirred by the social environment in either direction. This can read as profound calm or as disengagement depending on context; the book's framing suggests it's neither pretense nor deficiency, simply a nervous system receiving and amplifying comparatively little.

**Hyper-Attuned + Low-Reactive — "the steady reader"**
The book's own named anchor: a skilled therapist who perceives every nuance in a client's distress with extraordinary precision while remaining emotionally steady throughout. The antenna and the amplifier are explicitly decoupled here — full perceptual input, contained response — which the book treats as a distinct and valuable configuration rather than a lesser version of high reactivity.

**Hypo-Attuned + High-Reactive — "internally driven intensity"**
The book's own named anchor: someone who receives relatively little of the social signal around them, but what does get through produces a strong response — emotional intensity driven more by internal state than by the social environment. Can look, from outside, similar to Hyper-Attuned + High-Reactive, but the trigger for the intensity is different: internally generated rather than socially read.

## 12. Self-Orientation × Conscientiousness

**Altruism + Conscientious — "the systematic giver"**
Other-directed motivation channeled through discipline and planning — structural, systematic giving (building durable institutions or processes) rather than personal, case-by-case giving. Generosity here is sustained and scalable precisely because it's organized rather than dependent on the emotional peak of any single moment.

**Self-Focus + Impulsive — "chasing the next thing"**
Attention and resources center on the self, and follow-through on any given commitment (to self or others) is inconsistent — priorities shift with whatever the self wants right now rather than tracking toward a sustained personal or shared goal. Distinct from Self-Focus + Conscientious below in that even self-serving plans here are prone to abandonment.

**Altruism + Impulsive — "generous in the moment"**
Other-directed motivation without the structural follow-through to sustain it — genuine, spontaneous generosity (dropping everything to help) without the systems that would make the help durable or repeatable. This is close to Empathy + Impulsive (the responsive but unreliable helper) but the driver is priority-of-resources rather than moral resonance specifically.

**Self-Focus + Conscientious — "the disciplined self-builder"**
Attention centers on the self, pursued with real structure and follow-through — ambition, personal achievement, and long-term self-interest executed systematically. The book is explicit that self-orientation is not itself pathological; paired with conscientiousness this is often simply effective, sustained personal achievement, with the same caveat as elsewhere that the edge case is when others' interests stop counting as a constraint at all.

---

## 13. Self-Orientation × Reactivity

**Altruism + High-Reactive — "the urgent cause"**
Other-directed motivation combined with high emotional amplitude — a moral wrong doesn't just register as something to address eventually, it produces the kind of visceral disturbance the book describes as "fuel for art, activism, or reform." Generosity here can arrive with real force and urgency, at some risk of the same burnout pattern the book flags for high reactivity generally.

**Self-Focus + Low-Reactive — "unmoved and unmoving"**
Attention centers on the self, and little outside the self generates much amplitude either way. A calm, low-urgency profile in which other people's needs are unlikely to register with the force required to redirect priority, partly because the underlying orientation doesn't point outward and partly because not much registers with much force at all.

**Altruism + Low-Reactive — "principled and composed"**
Other-directed priority sustained without the intensity spikes that can burn out the high-reactive altruist — giving driven by reasoned principle rather than visceral anguish, combined with an even nervous system. This anchor is well-positioned for the kind of sustained, undramatic service the book associates with structural rather than emotionally-driven giving.

**Self-Focus + High-Reactive — "the volatile self"**
Attention centers on the self, and threats or slights to the self's status, image, or interests produce outsized emotional response. This is close to the book's account of vulnerable narcissism's fragility (hypersensitivity to criticism) amplified by a nervous system with a low threshold for activation — the reaction to a perceived slight can be disproportionate to the triggering event.

---

## 14. Conscientiousness × Agency

**Conscientious + Agentic — "the executive"**
Discipline and structure combined with comfort initiating and directing — plans are made and then actively driven forward rather than passively hoped for. This is close to what most organizations picture as strong leadership: decisive, systematic, willing to own outcomes.

**Impulsive + Accommodating — "carried along"**
Low structural follow-through and low assertive instinct combine into a profile that tends to go where circumstances (or other people) lead, without either a personal plan or the initiative to counter one imposed from outside. Not passivity exactly — genuine present-moment engagement can be real here — but direction tends to come from elsewhere.

**Conscientious + Accommodating — "the reliable second"**
Strong discipline and follow-through in service of someone else's direction rather than one's own — the book's exemplary-follower pattern (Kelley's "active and accommodating" description) combined with real structural competence. This is often the person actually executing a plan that someone more agentic set, and doing so more reliably than the agentic partner could alone.

**Impulsive + Agentic — "bold and unstructured"**
Comfort initiating and asserting, without the structural follow-through to sustain a given direction once momentum fades — decisive in the moment, prone to changing course as new impulses arrive. Can produce real creative or entrepreneurial leaps (comfort with risk, willingness to act on incomplete information) at the cost of consistent execution once the initial decisiveness has been spent.

---

## 15. Agency × Reactivity

**Agentic + High-Reactive — "the passionate leader"**
Comfort with initiative and assertion combined with high emotional amplitude — leadership or advocacy pursued with visible intensity, capable of mobilizing others through the force of the reaction itself, not just the position taken. Risk: the same intensity that mobilizes can also escalate conflict faster than a lower-amplitude leader would, and success can compound overconfidence, per the book's account of the arrogance feedback loop.

**Accommodating + Low-Reactive — "the unshaken deferrer"**
Comfort receding paired with a nervous system that isn't easily activated — a profile that neither seeks the lead nor is destabilized by much of anything. Genuinely stabilizing in group settings (the book's finding that even one accommodating, low-drama member improves team cohesion), at the cost of rarely supplying the urgency that would push a group to act.

**Agentic + Low-Reactive — "the calm commander"**
Directive and confident, without the amplitude that would make that direction volatile — the book's socialized-power pattern, calibrating assertiveness to context, decisive in emergencies without being destabilized by them. Closest to what the book describes as "flexible leadership": authority exercised without the emotional cost of high reactivity layered on top.

**Accommodating + High-Reactive — "the pressured deferrer"**
Discomfort with assertion combined with high emotional amplitude — the book's description of assertion itself feeling "stressful" for the accommodating end is intensified here: conflict or the prospect of it can produce real emotional flooding, even though the response to that flooding is typically further withdrawal rather than confrontation. This is the anchor most likely to absorb group tension internally at real personal cost, since the amplitude registers the strain even though the disposition doesn't act on it directly.

If a future editorial pass wants richer narrative texture in Layer 2, the safer route is invented, clearly fictional vignettes rather than real-person analogues.*
