# Empathy–Detachment item rubric

**Status:** adopted by the author (Albhy), September 20, 2026. Use it to write and review items for the Empathy and Detachment poles.

The definitions come from the private manuscript (see [../manuscript/README.md](../manuscript/README.md)) and are **paraphrased here without quotation. Do not add manuscript text to this file.**

**How to use it:** anyone (a person, Claude, Codex) reviewing or writing an Empathy or Detachment item applies the checks in section 3. If reviewers disagree on an item, the author decides. Agreement between reviewers is not approval.

---

## 1. Definition

Empathy is the **degree to which another person's suffering registers as real and
important to you and moves you to want to respond or relieve it.**
The guiding question for the axis is simply whether another person's suffering matters to you.

- **High:** others' suffering carries real weight and creates a pull toward
  responding. It is more than acknowledging that someone is in pain.
- **Low:** others' suffering carries little weight or motivation. It is treated
  as a fact to note, not as something that matters to the person. Low is not inherently
  pathological, and detachment can be adaptive in some roles.
- **Pathway-neutral:** concern can arrive through felt resonance or through
  reasoned recognition that the pain deserves a response. Two people with equal
  concern reached by different routes have the same Empathy.

## 2. What Empathy is NOT (scored elsewhere or not at all)

| Adjacent thing | Where it lives | Quick test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Noticing/reading others' signals | Social Attunement | Would someone oblivious to the suffering answer differently? Then it is perception, not concern. |
| Feeling what another feels (resonance) | Reactivity/Attunement; feeds Empathy but is not it | Is the item about the person's own emotion instead of the other's welfare? |
| Understanding another's inner state (perspective-taking) | Feeds Empathy but is not it | Does agreement show accuracy of understanding rather than wanting relief? |
| Who wins when interests compete; where resources and sacrifice go | Self-Orientation / Altruism | Does the item ask about priorities, giving, sacrifice or allocation? |
| Size/duration of one's own emotional reaction | Reactivity | Is intensity or volatility the point? |
| Planning, reliability, follow-through | Conscientiousness | Is the item about keeping commitments or effectiveness? |
| Duty, fairness, or principle as such | Values, not concern | Does the item rest on "I ought to" or "it's just" instead of "it matters / I want relief"? |
| Being overwhelmed by another's pain | "Empathic distress" (research term). In this framework it is not high Empathy. | Does the item reward being flooded or self-focused? |

## 3. Checks for every item (pass all)

1. **Object.** It is about the other person's suffering or wellbeing, not about an
   intervention, policy, cause, my duty, or my own feelings.
2. **Weight/motivation.** It captures that the suffering matters or that I want it
   eased, beyond noticing, understanding or attending to it.
3. **Route-neutral.** Someone whose concern always comes with strong feeling and
   someone who reaches it by reasoning, with equal concern, should endorse it about
   equally. Avoid making the *absence* of emotion a condition ("even when I feel
   nothing", "even when calm"). Avoid making the *presence* of emotion a condition
   ("only when it upsets me").
4. **Not conditional on an initial lack.** No "concern I did not feel at first."
5. **One construct.** No blending with attunement, duty, fairness, allocation or
   follow-through.
6. **Degree, not possibility.** Prefer "does / tends to" over "can". "I can care
   about X" measures capacity, which nearly everyone endorses, not how much.
7. **Wish to act, not acting.** Empathy is the *desire* to respond; Altruism is
   the behavior (doing for others as a matter of course). A strong wish counts even
   if the person lacks the means to act on it. Score the wish, not the deed, the
   means, or the outcome. Strength matters: a passing or weak wish is not enough
   for the high pole.
7a. **Plain "matters to me" language; secular, not religious.** Say the suffering
   matters to me or that I want it eased. "Moral" is fine: a secular person can be
   highly moral. Just don't let wording read as religious or doctrinal (sin,
   sacred, soul, divine, faith, "good person" judgments). Separately, keep
   "obligation", "duty", "ought" and self-sacrifice / "setting aside my own plans"
   out of Empathy items: those describe Altruism or duty, not the desire itself.
8. **Low-pole items describe little concern, not competence.** "I am more effective
   when I set emotion aside" describes regulation, not low Empathy. Low-pole wording
   should say the suffering carries little weight or little wish for relief.
9. **No moral verdict.** Neither pole should imply good or bad person.
10. **Plain and single-statement.** No double negatives; avoid "does not make…
    unimportant" style items, which test non-erosion.

## 4. Pool-level checks

- Mix of pathways across the pool, not only "concern despite no emotion".
- No clusters of near-duplicates (e.g. many "distance/strangers" items).
- A few items may test extension of concern across distance or group lines (the
  framework treats this as a real limit of empathy), but no single item should require it.

## 5. Worked examples (wording from the earlier empathy review)

| Item wording (abridged) | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| "the suffering involved has real weight for me" | Pass |
| "…even when I remain emotionally calm" | Fails check 3 (absence of emotion as a condition) |
| "draws my attention to their wellbeing" | Fails check 2 (attention, not motivation) |
| "a personal reason to care about it" | Fails check 1 (object is the intervention) |
| "a stronger wish to ease it" | Fails check 6 (measures sensitivity to vividness, not level) |
| "often feel little concern about what happens to them" | Pass |

## 6. Author decisions (Albhy, September 20, 2026)

1. **Wording:** plain "matters to me" (see check 7a). The word "moral" is allowed;
   it just must not read as religious.
2. **Persistence:** can be Empathy, Conscientiousness or Altruism. So a persistence
   item is fine only if it is clear which one it measures. For Empathy, it is the
   *concern or wish* that persists ("still matters to me after the feeling fades"),
   not the *commitment, follow-through or continued action* (Conscientiousness,
   Altruism). Avoid items that blur these.
3. **Distance and scale:** not yet decided. Treat as allowed but not
   required in any single item, per the pool-level checks.
4. **Altruism vs Empathy:** Altruism is the behavioral axis (doing for others as a
   matter of course). Empathy is the desire to act. A strong wish is enough; lacking
   the means does not lower it.
5. **Route balance:** yes. Deliberately balance feeling-led and reasoning-led items
   across the pool.
